Self-improving checker · 8-drawer chest · ShopBot Alpha PRS, Fellesverkstedet Oslo

Catch it before the router does.

A chest of drawers generated from parameters and cut from melamine sheets. Machine time costs about $53 an hour, so mistakes have to be caught in the files. Agents invent realistic faults, other agents write rules for the ones it misses, and an optimizer cuts cost without ever leaving what the checker allows.

0 · How it works, in plain words

An exam for the cutting files, and agents that keep making it harder

A program (the generator) turns a few numbers – width, height, number of drawers, board thickness – into cutting files for the CNC router and a parts list. A single wrong hole, depth or mirrored part wastes a sheet and an hour of machine time. This project builds a checker that reads those files and refuses to send a bad job, and then improves that checker on its own.

1

Break it on purpose

We write faults: small, realistic mistakes injected into a good design – a slide hole 12 mm too low, a pocket drilled through the face, an inch/mm mix-up in the file. Each fault is a test question with a known answer: “this must not be cut”.

65 practice (incl. 25 from the retired first test) · 25 unseen test · 7 known-good
2

Rules are plain code

A rule is a short Python check with a number and a reason from the workshop or a manufacturer: “Minifix housing in 19 mm board is 14.0 mm deep (Häfele p 7.13)”. No AI judges a design at check time – the same files always get the same answer.

31 rules · each one tested against every fault
3

The loop (RSI)

Recursive self-improvement, kept small and safe: one agent mines new faults from real failure modes, another writes a rule for one the checker misses, and a fixed gate keeps the rule only if it catches that fault, breaks nothing that was already caught, and raises zero false alarms on the known-good designs.

6 of 6 loop rules accepted · every one is a git commit
4

Honest scoring

The rule writers can read the training faults, so they will always ace those. The held-out faults (the unseen test) were written by a separate red-team agent and never shown to them – that score is the one that says whether the rules generalise. The known-good designs catch rules that are too strict.

Caught = stopped with an error. Warnings do not count.
5

Then optimise the cost

With a trustworthy checker, a hill-climbing optimizer can try hundreds of material and layout choices and keep only those the checker passes. The look stays fixed; only the hidden boards, column count and nesting change.

~370 designs tried in 35 s · Oslo and Bay Area, all in USD

3 · Found in the real chest

What the generator's own check let through

Fixed

Left and right parts were identical

The right side, middle wall and 8 right drawer sides were copies of parts that must be mirrored. Slide marks landed 30 mm too far back and the edge band ended up on the back edge. The drawers would not have closed square.

Caught by R04 handedness, R05 slide holes, R06 banding
Mirrored parts added (CH, SH)
Fixed

Holes deeper than the bit reaches

Ø5 handle holes, Ø4.5 plinth screws and slide pilots go 19 mm through, deeper than a short 1/8″ bit can cut.

Caught by R11 tool reach
CNC spots 3 mm, drilled through by hand
Fixed

Handle screws that barely bite

M4×40 through 37.6 mm of board leaves 2.4 mm of thread in the handle.

Caught by R09 fasteners
Changed to M4×45
Open decision

Drawers wider than the slide allows

Hettich's mounting sheet for the KA 4532 says 550 mm maximum. With two columns the boxes are 747 mm.

Found by the manual-reading agent · R18
Three columns or a heavier slide (priced below)
Fixed · from manuals

Minifix housing 0.5 mm too shallow

13.5 mm is Häfele's depth for 18 mm board. In 19 mm board the cam sits proud and the bolt head misses its ramp.

Caught by R19 · Häfele TCH 2023 p 7.13
Changed to 14.0 mm
Fixed · from manuals

Slide screws nearly through 16 mm sides

16 mm screws through a 1.2 mm runner leave 1 mm of board behind on 16 mm drawer sides; the melamine dimples on the inside.

Caught by R25 · Hettich MS 10547 p1
Ø4×14 as the manual says; hole pattern 37/165/229 and 37/165/291 entered
Fixed · from manuals

Groove feed too fast for a 1/8″ bit

With thinner hardboard the bottom groove drops under 6.35 mm and is cut with the 1/8″ bit at 3000 mm/min – Amana allows about 2030.

Caught by R21 · Amana compression spiral sheet
Groove feed set to 2000 mm/min

5 · The loop

How a rule earns its place

MineAn agent writes new faults from real failure modes: Hettich and Häfele manuals, CNC forums, cabinet practice.
ScoreGenerator plus every rule runs on each fault. The checks read the DXF files and the 3D assembly, not the intent.
WriteFor one missed fault, an agent writes one plain-Python rule with a workshop reason.
GateKept only if it catches that fault, loses nothing already caught, and raises zero false alarms on good designs.
