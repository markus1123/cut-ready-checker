Self-improving DFM checker · flat-pack on a ShopBot

Catch it before the router does.

An 8-drawer chest, generated from parameters and cut from melamine. Spindle time costs 568 kr/h, so every mistake should be caught in the files first. The checker gets better by itself: agents invent realistic faults, other agents write rules for the ones it misses, and a rule is kept only if it passes the gate.

Found in the real design

Three bugs the generator's own check passed

Left and right parts were identical

The right side, the middle wall and 8 right drawer sides were cut as copies of parts that must be mirrored. On the right side the slide marks land 30 mm further back and the edge band ends up on the back edge. The drawer would not close square.

R04 handedness · R05 slide holes · R06 banding
Fixed: mirrored parts CH and SH, middle wall face label corrected

Handle screws that barely bite

M4×40 through an 18.8 mm front plus an 18.8 mm drawer end leaves 2.4 mm of thread in the handle.

R09 fasteners
Fixed: M4×45 (7.4 mm engagement)

Holes deeper than the bit can reach

Ø5 handle holes, Ø4.5 plinth screws and the slide pilots need 19 mm through the board with a 1/8″ bit whose flute is typically 12.7 mm.

R11 tool reach
Fixed: CNC spots 3 mm deep, the holes are drilled through by hand
The loop

How a rule gets in

MineAn agent writes new faults from real failure modes: hardware manuals, CNC forums, cabinet practice.
ScoreGenerator plus all rules run on every fault. The DXFs and the 3D assembly are checked, not intent.
WriteFor one missed fault, an agent writes one plain-Python rule with a stated workshop reason.
GateKept only if it catches that fault, loses nothing already caught, and raises zero false alarms on known-good designs.
