Sheet 02MarketUnit price, log scale

Where the price gap is

Ukrainian troops have relied heavily on cheap commercial walkie-talkies that were never meant for a battlefield. Western military radios cost tens of thousands of dollars each. Himera already sells a jam-resistant handheld at roughly $370 and is now exporting to NATO.

So another handheld is not the opening. The open space is the network around the handheld: cheap relay nodes you can drop on a treeline, mount on a drone or leave in a basement, so squad radios keep a path back when one link is jammed. That is where volume is huge and current options cost thousands.

Prices marked est. are rough public ranges, not quotes. Verify with procurement contacts before the pitch.

Combine devices or build from scratch?

Pick an approach to see how it scores. Scores are 1–10, higher is better.

Recommendation: hybrid

Buy the hard, certified part (the RF transceiver module or SDR chip with its reference design). Build everything around it ourselves: carrier board, enclosure, battery pack, antenna, firmware and the network layer. Claude does most of its useful work exactly there: mechanical drawings, board outlines, BOMs, test procedures, and the rules that check them. Full scratch spends a year on RF and certification that Himera and others already solved. Pure combining caps how cheap and how rugged you can get.

Sheet 06MethodFrom the furniture checker

The AI design loop

Spec in plain text→ Claude writes CadQuery + KiCad→ Drawings, STEP, Gerbers, BOM→ Rule checker (plain code)→ Fix and redraw↺ Fault library grows
    Evidence the method works. The furniture checker (built today) went from catching 24% to 100% of training faults and from 4% to 72% of a blind held-out set, with 0 false alarms, by letting an agent write rules and keeping only those that pass a test gate. The radio version swaps drawer slides and Minifix holes for gaskets, RF keep-outs and drop tests.

    Claude drafts

    • Parametric enclosure (CadQuery/build123d) → STEP, 2D drawings with tolerances, 3D-print and CNC files
    • Carrier board schematic and layout from code (KiCad + atopile/SKiDL), around the module's reference design
    • BOM with alternates, assembly instructions, test and acceptance procedures
    • New rules + tests whenever a prototype fails in the lab or field

    Humans and instruments decide

    • RF matching, antenna tuning, emissions: measured with a VNA and spectrum analyzer, simulated in openEMS
    • Encryption and key handling: use an approved scheme, never home-made crypto
    • Field acceptance: soldiers' feedback is the final rule source
    • Rules stay plain code with tests, never a model's opinion of a drawing

    Sheet 08ToolingEgbe from RSI Hack #7

    Where Egbe fits

    Egbe is infrastructure for agents to run businesses: research, outreach email, ads, hosting, Stripe and analytics, organised as loops ("a decision you make more than once"). Its docs list no PCB-fab, CAD or parts-ordering integrations, so it should not run the design loop.

    It fits the business loop next to the design loop: finding grant and procurement calls, running supplier RFQ outreach, and turning unit feedback into spec changes. Humans stay on the loop and approve each outward message.

    Pricing: free to start, $10 credit, 15% fee on paid usage. Anything sent to the military or to suppliers needs human sign-off and nothing export-controlled in the prompts.

    Delivery plan

    Weeks 0–6 · Requirements

    Partner first

    • Find a Ukrainian partner through Brave1 and a volunteer fund. Get the real requirement from one brigade's signals officer.
    • Choose the RF module, test 3 on the bench.
    • Port the furniture checker: first 15 enclosure + board rules, seed fault library.
    ≈ $60k incl. starter lab
    Weeks 6–16 · Prototypes

    20 units

    • Loop generates enclosure + carrier board revisions daily. Print, assemble and measure in house.
    • Lab: range, battery life at −20 °C, drop, IP67.
    • Field trial with the partner unit, away from the front line.
    ≈ $80k
    Weeks 16–32 · Pilot

    500 units

    • EMS partner in Poland or Ukraine, first moulding tool.
    • Export licence (DEKSA in Norway) and Ukrainian codification.
    • Ship via partner fund, with spares, batteries and a 1-page field card.
    ≈ $120k + units
    Week 32+ · Scale

    10k / year

    • Firmware updates every few weeks as Russian EW adapts.
    • Every field failure becomes a new checker rule.
    • Funding: Brave1 grants, NATO DIANA, EU defence fund, donor procurement.
    ≈ $0.9–1.4M / yr at target cost

    Risks and what we do about them

    RiskWhy it mattersMitigation
    High Electronic warfare moves fastA radio that worked in spring can be useless by autumn.Software-defined module, field-updatable firmware, a front-line partner who reports what changes.
    High Export controlMilitary radios and crypto are controlled items. Shipping without a licence is a crime.Talk to DEKSA and a defence lawyer in week 1. Keep controlled details out of public repos and AI prompts.
    Med RF expertiseAI can draw a board; it cannot tune an antenna.Hire or partner with one experienced RF engineer. Measurements gate every rule.
    Med CompetitionHimera and others are ahead on handhelds.Start with relays that work alongside existing radios. Offer the AI design loop to Ukrainian makers as a service.
    Med Encryption approvalUnits will not trust unapproved crypto.Use the module's approved scheme or a partner's certified stack.
    Low ManufacturingEnclosures and carrier boards are routine for EMS houses.The checker's DFM rules mirror the fab's own limits.
